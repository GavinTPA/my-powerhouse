# Research Swarm — Guide

## Why This Matters

When you research a topic the normal way, you get one perspective. You search, read a few articles, and form an opinion based on whatever Google served first.

Research Swarm attacks the question from 3-5 angles simultaneously. Each angle is a separate researcher with a specific mandate. The magic happens in the synthesis — where do the angles agree? Where do they contradict? The contradictions are where the real insight hides.

Think of it like having five smart people in a room, each assigned a different lens to look through. You get answers no single search could find.

## When to Use It

Research Swarm is your go-to when:

- **"I need to understand X thoroughly"** — Technology evaluation, market research, competitive analysis
- **"What should I know about X before I build/teach/sell it?"** — Due diligence before commitment
- **"Research this for my newsletter/content"** — Finding angles and stories on a topic
- **"What are coaches doing with X?"** — Understanding the landscape of approaches

**Real examples:**
- "Research swarm on AI tools for client onboarding"
- "Deep dive on pricing models for group coaching programs"
- "I need to understand the podcast landscape for coaches"
- "Research how coaches are using Notion vs Obsidian for their systems"

## What to Expect

**Time:** 2-5 minutes (sub-agents run in parallel)

**Output:** A structured report with:
- Executive summary (the headline finding)
- Findings organized by research angle
- A contradictions table (where sources disagreed)
- Mission relevance section tailored to YOUR coaching business
- Action verdicts (act now / bookmark / ignore for each finding)
- Sources with links

**Cost:** Minimal — sub-agents use haiku (the fastest, cheapest model). The structure of the inquiry creates value, not raw compute.

## How It Gets Smarter With Your Context

If you've built your mission context (`/mission-context-builder`), the research angles adapt to your world:

- Instead of "How are people using this?" → "How would [your niche] coaches apply this?"
- Instead of "What's the market look like?" → "What does this mean for [your client avatar]?"
- The Mission Relevance section maps findings to YOUR practice, YOUR clients, YOUR delivery model

No mission context? It still works — you just get generic angles instead of personalized ones.

## Tips

- **Be specific:** "Research AI tools for client onboarding" gets better results than "research AI"
- **Add constraints:** "Research pricing models for group coaching, focused on programs under $2000/month"
- **Use "fresh research"** if you want to skip the vault check and go straight to web research
- **Follow up:** The Learning Triggers section at the end often suggests what to research next
