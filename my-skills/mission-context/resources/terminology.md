# My Terminology

Full terminology reference for The Profit Analyst — Gavin Smith's business, methodology, and tools.

---

## Core Framework

| Term | What It Means |
|------|---------------|
| **The Profit Analyst Method** | The overarching methodology. Three steps in order: Clarity → Confidence → Growth. Structured around decision-making for business owners — not accounting, compliance, or reporting. |
| **Clarity, Confidence and Growth** | The three phases of the client journey. Simplify → Clarity. Know → Confidence. Use → Growth. Always in that order — you can't skip steps. |
| **The Readiness Moment** | The trigger point when a business owner finally decides to stop outsourcing their financial understanding and take ownership themselves. The ideal client entry point. |
| **Use Your Numbers** | The core action — not "understand your finances", not "learn accounting." Use. Active, ownership-forward. Also the title of Gavin's book. |
| **Financial Ownership** | The idea that the understanding, reading, and decision-making from numbers must live with the owner — not the bookkeeper, not the accountant, not a part-time CFO. |

## The Finance Ecosystem

| Term | What It Means |
|------|---------------|
| **The Finance Ecosystem** | The three sets of numbers every business produces: P&L, Cash Flow, Balance Sheet. Each answers a different question and each is needed. |
| **P&L (Profit and Loss)** | Answers: "Are you winning or losing?" Income minus expenses over a period. The training record. |
| **Cash Flow** | Answers: "Can you stay alive?" The blood pulse of the business. Businesses don't fail because they're unprofitable — they fail because they run out of cash. |
| **Balance Sheet** | Answers: "What are you worth?" A snapshot at a point in time. Assets (Own) minus Liabilities (Owe) = Net Worth. The scales you step on at the end of the month. |
| **Owe and Own** | Plain-English replacement for liabilities and assets. Used to make balance sheet accessible without accounting jargon. |
| **Net Working Capital** | The cash flow engine — all current assets minus all current liabilities. The oil in the engine. |
| **Cash Conversion Cycle** | The time from money leaving the business to cash from customers coming back in. Shorter = less working capital needed. |

## The Profit Formula

| Term | What It Means |
|------|---------------|
| **The Profit Formula** | Price × Volume minus five expense categories = Profit. The seven variables that drive every business result. Comparing current vs. target formula reveals exactly what to focus on. |
| **The Seven Variables / Seven Numbers** | Price, Volume, Cost of Sales, Team, Marketing & Growth, Property, Overheads. Know these off the top of your head — they're the engine of your business. |
| **Price** | What you charge per transaction. A 10% price increase on a 10% margin business doubles profit — every extra dollar falls straight to the bottom line. |
| **Volume** | How many transactions you do. Interacts with price — you need both levers separately. |
| **Cost of Sales** | Direct costs of producing/delivering the product or service. Expressed as a ratio (% of revenue). The most commonly overlooked profit drain. |
| **Team** | All people costs — wages, contractor, super, payroll tax, benefits. Treated as fixed for the profit formula (moves in steps, not proportionally). |
| **Marketing and Growth** | All investment to grow the business — ads, coaching, business development. Evaluate by ROI. |
| **Property** | Premises costs — rent, outgoings. A significant fixed cost for bricks-and-mortar businesses. |
| **Overheads** | Everything else — insurance, phone, software, office, bank fees. Usually 10–15% of revenue. Rarely where the biggest opportunities are. |
| **The Power of One** | Moving each of the seven profit formula variables by just 1% produces a compounding effect that significantly exceeds a 7% profit improvement. |
| **Price Elasticity** | How sensitive customers are to price changes. Most business owners dramatically overestimate how elastic their market is — a 5–10% increase rarely moves the needle on volume. |
| **The Pricing Triangle** | Three approaches: Cost-Plus (add margin to cost), Market-Driven (price relative to competitors), Value-Based (price against the outcome created). Value-based is most powerful and least used. |
| **Price Volume Matrix** | Visual showing different combinations of price × volume that produce the same revenue. Reveals what kind of business you are and what strategy is right. |

## Cash Flow

| Term | What It Means |
|------|---------------|
| **Weekly Cash Flow Forecaster** | Tool 1. A weekly forward-looking view of cash — at least 13 weeks ahead — so owners always see the next low point in advance. Updated in 10 minutes every Monday. |
| **The Five Rules of Cash Flow Forecasting** | 1) Weekly not monthly. 2) Show actuals + forecast. 3) Always 13 weeks ahead. 4) Keep it simple (10–15 rows). 5) 80% accuracy is good enough. |
| **The Next Low Point** | The first question to answer every Monday: "When is our next low point and how low does it go?" Keeps you safe. |
| **Free Cash** | The second question: "How much cash is genuinely available for the next growth move?" Cash not earmarked for known expenses or buffer. This is your fuel. |
| **Champagne and Razor Blades** | The emotional rollercoaster of watching only the bank balance — high one week, crashed the next. Cash flow forecasting eliminates this. (From client Grant's description.) |

## Simplified Reporting

| Term | What It Means |
|------|---------------|
| **Five Expense Categories** | The simplified P&L structure: Cost of Sales, Team, Marketing & Growth, Property, Overheads. Collapses a 100-line P&L into six meaningful lines (plus revenue). |
| **The Monthly Clarity Report** | Tool 2. Monthly view of P&L and balance sheet in plain English — the numbers that matter, not an accounting dump. |
| **Ratio Analysis** | Expenses as a percentage of revenue — the "pizza" view. Allows comparison month-to-month regardless of revenue size. |
| **Trend Analysis** | Lining up multiple periods to see direction, not just position. A category growing from 32% to 40% over six months tells a different story than a single month's number. |
| **Fixed vs Variable Costs** | Fixed costs stay constant regardless of revenue (rent, most team). Variable costs move proportionally (cost of sales). Understanding this reveals why ratios behave differently. |
| **Break-Even Point** | Total fixed costs ÷ (1 − variable cost ratio). The minimum revenue needed to stay profitable. The most important single number in understanding current financial health. |

## Balance Sheet

| Term | What It Means |
|------|---------------|
| **The Four Balance Sheet Categories** | Net Working Capital, Long-Term Assets, Debt, Owner's Related Party. The simple lens for reading balance sheet movement. |
| **The Profit Bucket** | Model showing where profit goes: it flows into the working capital bucket, but three holes drain it — long-term asset purchases, debt repayments, and owner drawings. Losses drain from the bottom. |
| **Long-Term Assets** | Equipment, vehicles, fit-outs — wealth tied up in illiquid form. Buying these drains cash without changing net worth. |
| **Debt Repayments** | The second hole. Profit going to debt repayment reduces what's visible in the bank but improves net worth — the business is building value, not losing it. |
| **Owner Drawings** | The third hole. Money taken out of the business by the owner not recorded as wages. Often the hidden reason profit doesn't appear in the bank. |

## Growth and Strategy

| Term | What It Means |
|------|---------------|
| **The Profit Calculator** | Tool 3. Enter the seven profit formula variables — model scenarios in real time before committing. A flight simulator for the business. |
| **Five Year Forecast** | Tool 4. Long-term direction and impact of today's decisions on P&L and balance sheet. |
| **The Next Right Move** | The specific, data-backed answer to: "Given where we are and where we want to go, what single thing should we focus on this quarter?" Always comes from the profit formula. |
| **The Scaling Up Journey** | Peaks and valleys model — business growth is not a straight line. Peaks = capacity maxed, valley = investment before next level. Having numbers makes each valley manageable. |
| **The Drift Line** | From Simon Bowen's Four Futures model. The trajectory a business follows when the owner isn't using their numbers — starts small, compounds over time into missed opportunity or crisis. |
| **Simon Bowen's Four Futures** | Model: four possible business trajectories — Ideal, Good, Flat, Crisis. The drift line runs from average to flat/crisis. The gap from drifting vs deciding compounds over five years. |
| **Exit Formula** | Profit × Multiple = Exit Value. Both variables are within the owner's influence. |
| **What Drives Multiple** | Owner independence, recurring revenue, scalable systems, strong leadership team, brand/market position. The business that commands the highest multiple is the business you don't want to sell — it runs itself. |

## The Financial Operating System

| Term | What It Means |
|------|---------------|
| **The Four Rhythms** | The financial operating system: Weekly cash flow review (10 min), Monthly P&L + balance sheet review (30 min), Quarterly profit strategy session (2 hours), Biannual long-range forecast review (1 hour). |
| **The Six Strategic Questions / Six CFO Questions** | Two each from Cash Flow, P&L, Balance Sheet. Run on repeat — these are what a financially-led business owner asks every week, month, quarter. |
| **The Cash Flow Questions** | Q1: "When is our next low point and how low is it?" Q2: "How much cash is available for investment decisions?" |
| **The Profit Questions** | Q1: "What is my current profit formula?" Q2: "What is my target profit formula?" — comparing the two reveals exactly what to focus on. |
| **The Balance Sheet Questions** | Q1: "Where is my profit going?" Q2: "What is my current financial position and resources?" |
| **Quarterly Profit Strategy Session** | The most important meeting in the business calendar. Two hours, four questions in order: Results + position? Big goal? Next right move? Priorities + actions? |

## Client and Business Context

| Term | What It Means |
|------|---------------|
| **The Bell Curve of Financial Ownership** | Model: Ignorers (25%) → Numbers Aware (50%) → Know Their Numbers (20%) → Truly Using Their Numbers (5%). Success probability runs in a straight diagonal from left to right. |
| **The Player, The Scorekeeper, and The Referee** | Gavin's role analogy: The owner is the player (on the field making decisions). The bookkeeper is in the stands keeping score. The accountant is the referee (compliance, not strategy). The owner needs a coach — not a referee. |
| **$500K–$5M** | The revenue range of the ideal client in the book. The niche by problem (cash flow, profit understanding) not by sector. |
| **80% Is Good Enough** | A core principle — getting from 80% to 100% accurate takes disproportionate time relative to the decision-making value it adds. Move on 80%. Adjust as you go. |
| **Insights and Actions** | The purpose of numbers is not beautiful reports — it's knowing what to do next. Every review should end with at least one clear insight and one clear action. |
