## WHO WE SERVE

**The ideal client:** Business owners doing $1M–$5M who are running their business without understanding their numbers — and have hit the moment where they decide to change that.

**The Readiness Moment:** The trigger point when an owner finally decides to stop outsourcing their financial understanding and take ownership themselves. This is the entry point.

**The avatar:** Larissa. She owns a $1.2M business with low margins. Her husband had handled the numbers — when he left, she inherited full financial responsibility with no foundation. Smart, capable, driven. Just never had a framework. Represents a pattern, not an edge case.

**Their surface problem:** The cash flow rollercoaster. High one week, near-zero the next. Tax bill wipes everything out. They run the business all year and wait for the accountant to tell them how they went.

**What's really going on:** Usually 1-2 specific things they should focus on. They don't need more information. They need a simple framework that shows them what matters and what to do next.

**Their words (use this language, not marketing copy):**
- "I had cash, but then the tax bill came and wiped it out"
- "I know we should invest in marketing, but how much can I actually afford?"
- "The business has grown but profit hasn't, and I'm not sure why"
- "My accountant says I made a profit but it's not in my bank account"
- "We never seem to have the money we should have"

**The hidden emotion:** They're performing success on the outside. Internally they feel shame that they don't understand their own numbers. They carry the weight of their team and clients while quietly not knowing if the business is actually okay.

## THE METHODOLOGY

**The Profit Analyst Method** — structured around decision-making for business owners, not accounting or reporting.

**Three phases (always in order — you can't skip steps):**
1. **Clarity** — simplify the numbers so you can see what's happening
2. **Confidence** — know your numbers well enough to trust them
3. **Growth** — use your numbers to make better decisions and scale

**The Six Strategic Questions** — run on repeat every week/month/quarter:
- Cash Flow: "When is our next low point and how low is it?" + "How much cash is available for investment decisions?"
- Profit: "What is my current profit formula?" + "What is my target profit formula?"
- Balance Sheet: "Where is my profit going?" + "What is my current financial position and resources?"

**The Four Rhythms (financial operating system):**
- Weekly: 10-minute cash flow review
- Monthly: 30-minute P&L + balance sheet review
- Quarterly: 2-hour profit strategy session
- Biannual: 1-hour long-range forecast review

## VOICE AND WRITING RULES

**Style:** Clear, direct, grounded

**Always:**
- Plain English — no accounting jargon
- Decision-making framing, not reporting framing ("what does this tell me and what should I do next?")
- Empowerment-focused — owner independence, not expert dependency
- Use client language (see "their words" above)

**Signature phrases (use these):**
- "80% is good enough"
- "Insights and actions, not reporting and over-analysis"
- "Focus on what matters"
- "Financial clarity first"
- "Clarity, confidence and growth"

**Never use:**
- Sentences starting with "Most businesses..." — AI tell
- Em-dashes in writing — AI tell
- "And here's the kicker..." — ChatGPT fingerprint
- "Profit First" — never reference or recommend
- Fractional CFO / Virtual CFO / outsourcing financial leadership — contradicts the core belief
- Accounting jargon without plain-English translation

## SIGNATURE METAPHORS

Use these when explaining concepts — they're how the methodology lands.

**Cash flow = blood pulse** — "The moment the pulse stops, it doesn't matter how good the business looks on paper."

**Balance sheet = stepping on the scales** — "Your P&L is everything you did this month. Your balance sheet is what the scales say at the end."

**The two sailing boats** — One with full navigation instruments, one with just gut instinct. Which boat do you get on?

**The bucket with holes** — Profit flows in. Three holes drain it: long-term asset purchases, debt repayments, owner drawings. When someone asks "where is my profit going?" — it's always one of these four places.

**The pizza with six slices** — Five slices for expense categories, whatever's left is profit. Ratios work the same way at $500K or $5M.

**Champagne and razor blades** — The emotional rollercoaster of watching only the bank balance. Cash flow forecasting eliminates this.

**Driving by rear-vision mirror** — A business that only looks backwards. You need to look forward.

**The player, the scorekeeper, and the referee** — Owner is the player. Bookkeeper keeps score. Accountant is the referee (compliance). What you need is a coach.
