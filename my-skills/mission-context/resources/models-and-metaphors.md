# Models, Metaphors, and Stories

Gavin's signature analogies and real client stories. Use these when writing content in his voice — they're how he makes abstract financial concepts land for business owners.

---

## Signature Metaphors

**Cash flow = blood pulse**
"Your business can be healthy or sick — but what keeps it alive, regardless of health, is the pulse. The cash coming in and going out. The moment the pulse stops, it doesn't matter how good the business looks on paper. It's over."

**Balance sheet = stepping on the scales**
"Your P&L is everything you did over the past month — all the training, all the eating, all the decisions. Your balance sheet is what the scales say when you step on them at the end. It's the result captured in a single snapshot."

**The two sailing boats**
Two identical boats preparing to cross the Pacific. One has full navigation instruments — GPS, compass, daily weather, resource tracking updated daily. The other has just the skipper's gut. Which boat do you get on? Running a business without your numbers is the second boat.

**The player, the scorekeeper, and the referee**
The owner is the player on the field. The bookkeeper is in the stands with a notepad, keeping score (not making decisions). The accountant is the referee — their job is compliance, not strategy. Your accountant doesn't care whether your profit margin is 10% or 20%. You should. What you need is a coach.

**The pizza with six slices**
Six slices regardless of the size of the pizza. Five slices for the five expense categories. Whatever's left is yours — that's profit. Ratios work the same way whether you're a $500K or a $5M business.

**Polishing hubcaps while the engine is on fire**
When a business owner spends energy cutting overheads (phone bills, subscriptions) while cost of sales is bleeding out 60% of revenue. Sherry the jeweller — she was making the workplace miserable cutting small costs while the real problem sat untouched.

**Champagne and razor blades**
Grant's description (25-year marketing agency owner): "One week the bank balance is high and you feel like a genius. The next week it's crashed and you're lying awake wondering if you made the worst decision of your life." Bank balance watching builds financial PTSD. The cash flow forecast fixes this.

**The drift line**
From Simon Bowen's Four Futures model. The trajectory a business follows when the owner isn't using their numbers. The gap from the drift line to the ideal looks small at the start — but it compounds. Five years of drift vs five years of number-led decisions is the difference between half a million dollars of profit and over a million.

**The bucket with holes**
Net working capital is a bucket. Profit flows in from the top. Three holes on the side drain it: 1) long-term asset purchases, 2) debt repayments, 3) owner drawings. Losses drain from the bottom. When someone says "where is my profit going?" — it's always one or more of these four places.

**The commercial aircraft correcting course**
A Sydney-to-London flight is off course almost the entire journey. Wind, weather, ATC diversions. None of it matters — because the system checks position, calculates the correction, adjusts, and repeats hundreds of times per hour. The plane lands on time. Not because it flew a perfect line. Because it had a system. This is how the financial operating system works.

**The runner who won't get on the bike**
Response to "I don't have time." A runner straining to cover ground, exhausted. Someone pulls up on a bike and offers it. "Can't stop — too busy running." That's what telling me you don't have time actually means: you're spending time on the wrong things because you don't yet know what the right things are.

**The lever and the fulcrum**
A business owner straining to push a large rock — every muscle, barely moving. Someone else applies minimal effort with a lever and fulcrum. That's the difference between working hard and working smart. Most business owners are exhausted because they're pushing on the wrong part of the rock.

**Driving by rear-vision mirror**
A business that only looks backwards — only reviews what already happened. Looking at a P&L and asking "how did we do?" is necessary. But you don't drive a car with your eyes on the rear-vision mirror. You glance at it. Then you look forward. Forecasting is looking forward.

## Named Models

| Model | What It Does |
|-------|-------------|
| **The Finance Ecosystem** | Shows how P&L, Cash Flow, and Balance Sheet are three separate lenses on the same business — each answering a different question. |
| **The Clarity, Confidence, Growth Pyramid** | Visual of the three-step method. You can't skip to growth. |
| **The Bell Curve of Financial Ownership** | Ignorers (25%) → Aware (50%) → Know (20%) → Using (5%). Success probability is a straight diagonal from left to right. |
| **Simon Bowen's Four Futures** | Four trajectories from now to five years: Ideal, Good, Flat, Crisis. The drift line compounds. |
| **The Scaling Up Journey** | Peaks and valleys — not a straight line. Each peak hits a constraint; investment creates a valley; then a new peak. Visibility makes each valley manageable instead of terrifying. |
| **The Price Volume Matrix** | Different combinations of price × volume that produce the same revenue. Same destination, completely different businesses and strategies. |
| **The Pricing Triangle** | Cost-Plus, Market-Driven, Value-Based. Most businesses use cost-plus. Value-based is most powerful and least common. |
| **The Pizza Diagram** | Six slices: five expense categories + profit. Ratios work regardless of pizza size. |
| **The Profit Formula** | Price × Volume − (CoS + Team + M&G + Property + Overheads) = Profit. The seven variables. |
| **The Power of One** | 1% improvement across each of the seven variables produces a compounding result far larger than 7%. Shows the leverage in the formula. |
| **The Break-Even Chart** | Fixed costs flat line, variable costs slope up, income line crosses both — the crossing point is break-even. Below = loss. Above = profit. |
| **The Profit Bucket** | Net working capital as a bucket. Profit in from top, three holes on the side (assets, debt, drawings), losses drain the bottom. |
| **The Four Balance Sheet Categories** | Net Working Capital, Long-Term Assets, Debt, Owner's Related Party. The simple monthly lens. |
| **The Six CFO Questions** | Two pairs each for Cash Flow, P&L, Balance Sheet. The recurring rhythm that keeps a business financially led. |
| **The Four Rhythms** | Weekly (10 min), Monthly (30 min), Quarterly (2 hours), Biannual (1 hour). The financial operating system. |
| **The Exit Formula** | Profit × Multiple = Exit Value. Both variables within the owner's influence. |

## Client Case Studies

These are real clients from Gavin's book. Use when writing case study content or examples.

**Larissa** — Industrial components business. Her husband had always handled the numbers. When the marriage ended, the business stayed with her and she had no idea if it was worth keeping. Through the Profit Analyst Method she went from "do I even keep this?" to making confident hiring, marketing, and software decisions — all backed by the numbers. She's Gavin's ideal client avatar.

**Grant** — Marketing agency owner, 25+ years. Coined "champagne and razor blades." Smart, experienced, never able to make his financials mean anything. First time his numbers were shown as a colour-coded chart: "This is financials for creatives. I get it now."

**Sherry** — Jeweller. Classic "polishing hubcaps" story. Was cutting every overhead she could find — phone bills, software, making the workplace miserable. Her overheads were 15% of revenue. Her cost of sales were 60% — and she wasn't tracking wastage, had no pricing standards, no supplier negotiation. Moving CoS by 2% was worth double the effort of cutting all overheads by a third.

**Hairdresser** — Unnamed. Fully booked with a month-plus waitlist. Hadn't raised prices in years. Numbers made it obvious: demand far outstripping supply, capacity maxed, only lever available was price. Raised prices, profit improved dramatically, waitlist shortened. "The fear of raising prices is one of the most expensive fears a business owner can carry."

**Amanda** — Car detailing business, North Brisbane. At capacity in their current premises. Taking a second lease next door felt terrifying — doubling property costs. Profit formula showed the incremental revenue would flow through at a much higher rate than the overall margin, because most fixed costs were already covered. They took the lease. Volume grew. Constraint solved. Next constraint (team) identified and addressed. Business scaled sequentially.

**Owen** — Industrial painting, central Queensland, 15 years. Mid-50s, thinking about what's next. Current exit value: ~$1M. Too much of the business ran through Owen personally (low multiple). Roadmap: grow profit deliberately + build a leadership layer + strengthen systems = forecast exit value of $4–5M over five years. Went from "vaguely thinking about winding down" to energised and focused.

**Ben (forklifts)** — Gold Coast. Business was profitable on the P&L, but he never felt like he was getting ahead. Balance sheet showed why: almost all monthly profit was going straight out the debt hole (equipment finance repayments). Not lost — going somewhere real. Once he could see the debt coming down, he stopped feeling like something was wrong and started feeling like something was working.

**Ben (flooring)** — Melbourne. COVID debt, deeply negative working capital. $10K profit/month against significant debt. Built a 10-month countdown: month by month, the debt reducing to zero. "Seeing the ten-month runway was the thing that changed my mindset completely. I finally had headspace again." Forecasting turned the fog into a map.

**Laura** — Tennis club with her husband. Business generating $15K profit/month. Owners taking $20K/month in drawings — not as wages, just transfers when they needed it. Every month: $5K backwards. Significant negative position building in owner's related party. She had no idea the drawings were exceeding profit. Fix: formalise wages, bring drawings in line with profit. Simple once visible.

## Gavin's Own Business Story

**The Beach Bars (thebeachbars.com.au) — Sunshine Coast**
- Started with Kings Beach Bar. Year 1: $1.1M revenue, losing $150K/year.
- Over 7 years: grew into a group of 3 bars doing $11.5M revenue, $1.5M annual profit.
- Not a straight line. The scaling up journey in action: peaks, valleys, constraints solved one after another.
- The turning point: 18 months in, breaking even at $1.5M revenue. Profit formula showed that $500K more revenue would only cost $200K more in expenses — $150K CoS + $50K labour. Were spending nothing on marketing. Modelled $100K marketing spend → $500K revenue → $200K profit. Signed up. Six months later: $2M revenue, $200K profit. Business turned the corner.
- Approach: identify constraint → model investment in profit formula → validate return → make the move. Repeated every quarter for seven years.

**Earlier background**
- First jobs out of school: watched two businesses fail in quick succession. A travel agency (cash flow killed it before it had a chance). A convenience store/café owned by a police couple who put their life savings in — watched it die quietly over 12 months. "Their savings were gone. We as employees didn't receive our superannuation." Went back to university after that with a completely different attitude toward finance.
- KPMG: qualified chartered accountant, auditor. Learned to read businesses from the inside out.
- 10 years commercial: analyst → financial controller → CFO for a large global marketing agency operating across 60 countries. Hundreds of businesses reporting in.
- Then: left the boardrooms, moved back to the Sunshine Coast to be close to family. Bought a café with 10 employees and started a Pilates studio from scratch simultaneously.
- Insight that led to the coaching business: joined a business coaching program for help with the Pilates studios and realised finance was the biggest weak point — not just for him, but for almost every business owner in the room. "Smart, driven people who had built real businesses and were completely in the dark about their own numbers. Most of them suffering in silence, because there's a quiet shame that comes with not understanding this stuff when you're supposed to be in charge."
- 1,000+ clients across 40+ industries: trades, health, hospitality, professional services, construction, retail.
