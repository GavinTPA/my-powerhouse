# Mission Context Examples

Show these examples AFTER the member completes each section. They demonstrate what "great" looks like — specific, differentiated, and genuinely useful for downstream AI tools.

---

## Example Coach: Sarah Chen

**Niche:** Operational efficiency for creative agencies (design, video, branding firms doing $500K-$3M)

---

### WHO I AM - Example

```
## WHO I AM

**Sarah Chen** - Operations coach for creative agency owners who are brilliant at the craft and drowning in the business.

**Niche:** Creative agencies (design, video, branding) doing $500K-$3M who've hit the ceiling where the founder can't be in every project anymore.

**What clients get:** 12-month group program with monthly 1:1 strategy sessions. I audit their current workflows, map where founder-dependency lives, and build the playbook that lets them step out of production without quality dropping.

**Superpower:** I can look at a creative agency's project flow and spot exactly where the founder is the bottleneck in under 30 minutes. Most ops consultants want to systematize everything. I find the 2-3 choke points that, once cleared, unlock the whole machine.

**Revenue:** $400K/year
```

**What makes this good:**
- Niche isn't "business coaching" — it's a specific type of business, at a specific stage, with a specific problem
- Superpower is a capability ("I can spot where the founder is the bottleneck") not a character trait ("I'm really empathetic")
- Delivery model is concrete — you know exactly what a client gets

### MY CLIENTS - Example

```
## MY CLIENTS

**Ideal Client:** Marcus runs a 9-person branding agency. He started as a designer, built the team around his talent, and now he's the creative director, project manager, and quality control department all in one. Revenue hit $1.2M last year and he should be thrilled. Instead, he's working 60-hour weeks, reviewing every deliverable, and his best designer just quit because there was no room to grow.

**Surface problem:** "I need better systems and processes" — that's what Marcus says on the first call.

**Deeper problem:** Marcus doesn't have a systems problem. He has a trust problem. He built the agency on his creative standard and he's terrified that without his eyes on everything, the work will slip. The systems he needs aren't project management tools — they're decision-making frameworks that let his team own quality without him.

**Success looks like:** It's a Thursday afternoon. Marcus is at his daughter's soccer game. His phone buzzes — the team just sent a campaign to the client for review. He glances at it, smiles, puts his phone away. He doesn't need to fix anything. Six months ago, he would have pulled over to rewrite the headlines. Today, the team nailed it because they have the same quality filters he used to keep in his head.

**Their words:**
- "I can't clone myself"
- "I'm the bottleneck and I know it"
- "Every time I step back, quality drops"
- "I hired good people but I can't let go"
- "I don't need more clients, I need more capacity"

**Where they hang out:** Agency-specific Slack communities (Bureau of Digital, Agency Collective), listen to 2Bobs podcast, attend Owner Summit conference
```

**What makes this good:**
- Marcus is a person with a name, a situation, and specific details — not "agency owners aged 35-50"
- Two-layer problem: surface ("systems") vs. real ("trust / letting go of creative control")
- Success is a visible moment you can picture, not a metric
- Language is raw — these are things real people say, not marketing copy

### MY VOICE - Example

```
## MY VOICE

**Style:** Direct, warm, systematic

**How I communicate:**
- I lead with the structure, then fill in the emotion
- I use visual metaphors — flowcharts, maps, blueprints
- I break big problems into numbered sequences
- I validate the struggle before offering the solve

**Signature phrases:**
- "Let's map this out"
- "Where does this break without you?"
- "You built something worth protecting — now let's protect it without you standing guard"
- "What would happen if you just... didn't check?"
- "The system should catch that, not you"

**Words I avoid:**
- "Scale" (overused, means nothing specific)
- "Leverage" without context (too vague)
- "Hustle" or "grind" (opposite of what I teach)
- "Mindset" as a standalone solution
- "Empower" (corporate fluff)

**Influences:**
- Mike Michalowicz (Clockwork — the whole "design to be replaceable" philosophy)
- Gino Wickman (EOS / Traction — but I strip out the parts that don't work for creative businesses)
- Brene Brown (the vulnerability stuff, specifically around why founders can't delegate)
- David Allen (GTD principles applied to team workflows, not personal productivity)
```

**What makes this good:**
- Three words capture the style (Direct, warm, systematic) and they're specific — not "professional and friendly"
- Signature phrases are things a client would quote back. You could write in this voice without meeting Sarah.
- Words to avoid are specific and have reasons. "Scale" isn't banned because it's bad — it's banned because it's meaningless without context.
- Influences have context — not just names, but what specifically she takes from each one

### MY TERMINOLOGY - Example

```
## MY TERMINOLOGY

| Term | What It Means |
|------|---------------|
| **The Bottleneck Map** | Visual audit of every point in a project where the founder must personally touch the work. Usually reveals 2-3 critical choke points, not the 15 they think exist. |
| **Quality Filters** | Decision-making criteria that used to live only in the founder's head, now documented so the team can self-check work before it goes to clients. Not SOPs — these are judgment frameworks. |
| **The Thursday Test** | Can you take Thursday afternoon off without anything breaking? Used as the benchmark for operational readiness. Named because Thursday is the day most agency fires happen. |
| **Founder Ghost** | When the founder has technically stepped back from a role but still reviews, second-guesses, or "just checks in on" every output. The role is filled on paper, empty in practice. |
| **Capacity Debt** | The gap between what a team could produce and what they actually produce because founder-dependency creates idle time. Like technical debt, but for human throughput. |
| **The Handoff Protocol** | Structured process for transferring a responsibility from founder to team member. Includes shadowing, guided practice, solo with review, and full ownership phases. |
| **Revenue Ceiling** | The revenue level where founder-dependency maxes out. Usually $1-1.5M for creative agencies. Breaking through requires operational change, not more hustle. |
```

**What makes this good:**
- Every term is genuinely unique to Sarah's world — not generic coaching terms anyone could claim
- Definitions are specific enough that AI tools could use them correctly in context
- Terms build a cohesive vocabulary — they relate to each other and paint a picture of her methodology
- 7 terms (minimum 5 required) — enough to establish the language without padding

## Quick Checklist: Good vs. Needs Work

| Section | Good | Needs Work |
|---------|------|------------|
| **WHO I AM** | "Operations coach for creative agencies doing $500K-$3M" | "I coach business owners" |
| **Niche** | Differentiates from 10 other coaches in the room | Could describe half the coaches you know |
| **Superpower** | A capability: "I can spot X in 30 minutes" | A trait: "I'm really passionate about helping people" |
| **Client avatar** | A person with a name, situation, and specific struggle | A demographic: "entrepreneurs aged 30-50" |
| **Two-layer problem** | Surface: "I need systems" / Real: "I can't let go of control" | Single layer: "They want to grow" |
| **Success moment** | "Thursday afternoon at his daughter's soccer game..." | "They feel more confident" |
| **Client language** | "I can't clone myself" | "They desire operational excellence" |
| **Voice style** | "Direct, warm, systematic" | "Professional and friendly" |
| **Signature phrases** | Things clients quote back to you | Generic advice anyone might say |
| **Words to avoid** | Specific words with reasons | "Bad words" or nothing |
| **Terminology** | Unique to their world, with clear definitions | Generic industry terms |
