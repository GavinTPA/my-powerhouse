Profit Gameplan Calls:
Who Buys and Who Stalls

What 13 sales calls in October 2026 tell us about our ideal client

What we looked at. Every 45-Min Profit Gameplan call from 8 to 26 October 2026: 4 who bought on the call (Adam, Suzie, Rachel, Xavier) and 8 who did not (Alex, Raj, Ashita, Michael, Brad, Clayton, Phillip, Kaveh). Seven of the eight landed in "not now, maybe January". One said no and hired a local hands-on CFO. The offer was the same every time: the 90-day program at $8,800 paid in full or $880 a week.

The short answer

Buys on the call

  • One owner carries the money alone and makes the call
  • Profit is thin or gone, even with healthy revenue
  • Live tax pain: ATO debt or a BAS bill that empties the account
  • People-heavy service business, wages the biggest cost
  • Only help is an accountant who looks backwards
  • Wants to understand the numbers, not hand them off
  • Has hit "enough is enough"

Stalls at "maybe January"

  • A partner or co-owner shares the decision and is not on the call
  • Profitable and fairly comfortable
  • Already has a forecast, CFO, coach or franchise support
  • Wants someone to do it for them
  • Bigger, multi-site or multi-entity, mid-expansion
  • Came mainly to pay less tax

Who our buyers are

  1. They carry the money alone. Partners, siblings and managers defer to them, so they can say yes on the call. "I'm the one that steers the ship." "Quite lonely."
  2. The tax bill is the wound. Three of four had ATO debt ($400k, $200k, and a payment plan after a $35k bill). The fourth watched the BAS drain the account. It was the most emotional moment of every call.
  3. Revenue is fine, profit has gone missing. All four sat near break-even. Two used to make good money and watched it slide. "How am I profitable, but I have no money in the bank?"
  4. They have outgrown their accountant, but do not want to fire them. "The accountant will keep you out of prison. The bookkeeper will keep everything clean. You need someone to move to the next step."
  5. People-heavy businesses. Wages drive the cash rollercoaster. Two raised the same pattern unprompted: four-week months make money, five-pay-week months lose it.
  6. Self-taught operators, great at their trade. "I'm not a numbers person." "I never got any management schooling."
  7. A life pressure point pushed them over the line. A parent's stroke, a family health crisis, being the sole income earner, a business that "swallowed me whole". Two said "enough is enough" almost word for word.
  8. They have a specific thing to build and no map to it. New trucks and a GM, a saleable business, supported-living homes, a commercial shed.
  9. Price was never the objection, cash timing was. All four chose $880 a week. The cash flow guarantee closed the last gap.
  10. They arrived warm. All four had a setter call first. The sales call confirmed fit; it did not have to create the need.

Revenue ranged from $600k to $6M across buyers, so two sit outside our $1M to $5M band. The pain profile predicted the close better than revenue did.

Seven differences

  1. One decision maker. The strongest signal. All four buyers decided alone. Six of eight non-buyers shared the decision, and five ended on "I'll talk to them". The partner was rarely on the call.
  2. Buyers were hurting now; non-buyers were mostly fine. Buyers sat near break-even with live ATO pain. Non-buyers were mostly profitable (10% to 60%) and came from curiosity, exit planning or a goals list. The one non-buyer with live ATO pain, Brad, is the one saying "we need this".
  3. Buyers had nothing else; non-buyers had a substitute. An AI cash forecast, Fathom and franchise managers, turnaround advisers, buying-group coaching, their own accounting training, $60k of past coaches. With the slot filled, our program feels optional.
  4. Buyers wanted to learn it; non-buyers wanted it done, or already did it. "Is there mentoring in there... or is it just figures?" "Do you know roughly what a face-to-face CFO would cost?" Our "don't stay reliant on an expert" line wins learners and pushes away delegators.
  5. Non-buyers were bigger, more complex and mid-expansion. New sites, a rebrand, an accountant switch, a new investor. Four of eight were in hospitality or accommodation; none of the buyers were.
  6. Timing was the excuse, not the cause. Buyers had terrible timing too and bought anyway. "At the time it happens, it's never the right time."
  7. Price was never the objection; payment shape was. One balked only at "that initial 90-day chunk", another wanted to pay in full over two or three invoices, and one never heard the real price.

Small sample: 12 prospects, 13 calls. Treat these as strong signals, not proven rules. Most gaps between the groups are wide.

What we do about it

Fix in the sales process
  • Get both decision makers on the call. When the setter hears a partner shares money decisions, book both.
  • Price earlier. It landed 50 to 70 minutes in, and one prospect never heard it.
  • Shorter walkthroughs. Long demos got "Yeah" in reply. Calls ran to 95 minutes.
  • Book the next step before hanging up. Most calls ended with a link in chat and nothing in the calendar.
  • Screen out tax-cutting enquiries at the setter stage.
Live pipeline
  • Brad looks most like a buyer. The block is his business partner. Get him on a call.
  • Kaveh asked to pay in full over two or three invoices. Saying yes may be all it takes.
  • Phillip is "sold", but his partner is sole director. She needs her own call, not just the recording.
