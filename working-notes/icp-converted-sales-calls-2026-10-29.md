Small sample (four calls). Treat the patterns as strong signals, not proof.

## The common thread

### 1. They are the one person carrying the money in their head
Every buyer is the sole financial decision maker, and nobody else in the business shares the load. Partners, siblings and managers defer to them. This is why each one could say yes on the call with no "I need to talk to my partner" stall.
- Adam: "at the end of the day, I'm the one that steers the ship"
- Suzie: "she'll listen to whatever I suggest"
- Rachel: "it's not something I really particularly discuss with [my manager]... quite lonely"
- Xavier: "I'm supporting six people"

### 2. The tax bill is the wound
Three of four carry ATO debt, and the fourth (Suzie) describes the BAS draining the account. The ATO is the most emotional moment in every call. It is the clearest outward proof that "profit on paper, nothing in the bank" is real for them.
- Adam: "We can't afford to pay the BAS... that's the keeping awake at night"
- Xavier: "they come with numbers that don't match what I see"
- Suzie: "a tax man took it all"
- Rachel: $200k ATO debt, "I honestly really stressed about this"

### 3. Revenue is fine, profit has gone missing
All four sit at or near break-even despite a decent top line. Two of them (Suzie, Xavier) used to make good money and watched it slide without knowing why. Their own words match Gavin's ad language almost exactly.
- Suzie: "how am I profitable, but I have no money in the bank?"
- Xavier: "Mr Taxman says I've made that much profit... it's not there. I don't know where it is."
- Rachel: "just sort of scraping by"

### 4. They already pay an accountant and bookkeeper, and have hit the ceiling of that
None want to fire the accountant. They have worked out the accountant looks backwards, after the fact, for compliance. The gap they feel is "someone to tell me what to do next". Xavier's client put it perfectly: "The accountant will keep you out of prison. The bookkeeper will keep everything clean. You need someone to move to the next step."
- Adam: accountant was meant to help with budgeting, "it feels like it dropped off"
- Rachel: with the accountant "you sort of feel like a bit of a dum-dum"
- Xavier: accountant minimises tax "but it's only after the fact"

### 5. People-heavy service businesses where wages drive the rollercoaster
Wages are the biggest cost in all four. Adam and Suzie both describe the exact same pattern: four-week months look profitable, five-week months lose money. Xavier pays his staff "religiously" before himself. Rachel's costs are ~70% direct labour.

### 6. Self-taught operators, brilliant at their craft, never shown the numbers
- Adam: left school end of Year 10, straight into a van. "I never got any management schooling"
- Xavier: "I'm not a numbers person... school was 35 years ago"
- Rachel: "I would definitely mark myself as a slower learner", "I don't do technology very well"
- Suzie: more savvy but "I'm second guessing myself"

### 7. A personal pressure point tipped them into the readiness moment
Each call has a life event or a line in the sand behind it. Two said "enough is enough" almost word for word.
- Suzie: on the call from her dying father's nursing home. "Enough's enough. It's time for us to be business owners"
- Xavier: "I'm at a moment where, okay, enough is enough"
- Adam: his father (70) had a stroke. "I don't want to end up like that." Doesn't want his kids inheriting the business as it is
- Rachel: "it's definitely swallowed me whole". Wants her life back as a mum

### 8. They have a concrete thing they want to buy or build, and no map to get there
Not vague "growth". A specific asset or exit: new trucks and a GM (Adam), a saleable business (Suzie), SIL homes and a hub (Rachel), a commercial shed (Xavier). Gavin's "1% of margin is worth $X" and the bar story connected because it showed a path to that thing.

### 9. They already spend money on help
Rachel spent $30k on a consultant and had shopped for an offshore CFO. Suzie paid a marketing company and an Airtasker contractor. Adam sat a call with Aureus. Xavier has an accountant and bookkeeper. Price never raised an objection. Cash timing did, which is why all four took weekly payments and why the guarantee mattered (Rachel: "in two weeks you can tell me if it's not a good idea").

### 10. Warm before they arrived
All four had a setter conversation first (Connor or Melinda), and Adam also got a personalised video ("you've gone out of your way twice"). They arrived already believing they had the problem. The call confirmed fit, it didn't create the need.

## What landed on the call
- **Gavin as an owner, not just an accountant.** Suzie: "being that you've been a business owner... sometimes you talk to people who've never actually had a business"
- **The bars story.** Xavier: "Wow." Suzie: "Can you do that for me?"
- **Industry proof.** Suzie (Pilates, Best Body CFO), Rachel (NDIS client Kate, SeaChange)
- **The coach / referee / scorekeeper analogy.** Xavier: "And I think that's what I need"
- **Weekly payment plus the cash flow guarantee.** Removed the only real risk they felt

## Their questions (not objections)
- "Will I be dealing with you, or a junior?" (Suzie)
- "How do you actually implement this?" (Rachel)
- "When do we start, I'm flat out until Christmas" (Xavier)
- "As long as you feel I'm a correct person" (Suzie)

## Revenue band note
Revenue ranged from $600k to $6M. Two of four sit outside the $1M-$5M band, yet all closed easily. The pain profile (sole decision maker, ATO pain, break-even, people-heavy, readiness trigger) predicted the close better than revenue did.

## Implication flagged for the Intensive
All four buyers were cash-tight and chose to pay weekly. An upfront $7,500 (or $3,750) in-person ticket may meet more resistance from this exact profile than the $880/week option did. Worth testing a payment plan or guarantee in the Intensive offer.

## Buyers vs non-buyers: what's different

### 1. The money decision sat with one person (strongest signal)
- Buyers: 4 of 4 were the sole decider and said yes on the call.
- Non-buyers: 6 of 8 had a second person in the decision (Penny, Carla, Jess, Annie, Ash, Luke). Five of those six ended on "I'll talk to them". Only once was the partner on the call (Ashita), and even then the second owner was "new to this".
- The three solo non-buyers (Alex, Clayton, Ramy) stalled for other reasons (never heard the offer; upfront lump; no money pain). Deciding alone is necessary, not enough.

### 2. Buyers were hurting now. Non-buyers were mostly doing fine
- Buyers: break-even or close (Adam -$20k, Suzie ~$0, Rachel 5%, Xavier break-even), and 3 of 4 had live ATO debt.
- Non-buyers: mostly profitable (Raj ~60%, Michael ~20%, Clayton $180k, Alex/Phillip/Kaveh ~10%). Only Brad has live ATO pain, and he's the one still saying "we need this". Ramy: ~$180k per rink, tax up to date, "No, we are good" on cash.
- Ramy's trigger was real but about his time (60-80 hr weeks, "the roadblock is probably myself"), not money.
- Non-buyer triggers were strategic or curious (exit planning, a goals list, a podcast, a tax bill they can pay). Buyer triggers were "enough is enough".

### 3. Buyers had nothing else. Non-buyers already had a substitute
- Buyers' only help was an accountant who looks backwards.
- Non-buyers already had something sitting in the slot: Michael's AI forecast, Clayton's Fathom + franchise managers, Brad's turnaround advisers, Ashita's buying-group coaching (then a CFO), Kaveh's own accounting training, Alex's $60k of past coaches, Ramy's personal coach.

### 4. Buyers wanted to learn it. Non-buyers wanted someone to do it, or already did it
- Buyers: "I want to know my numbers", "hold my hand for a while" (until they can do it).
- Non-buyers split two ways. Do it for me: Ashita, Raj, Kaveh, Phillip, Brad ("I bring specialists in"), Alex ("mentoring"). Already doing it: Michael.
- Gavin's pitch against CFOs ("you're still reliant on the expert") lands with a learner and pushes away a delegator. Ashita is the clearest case.

### 5. Non-buyers were bigger, more complex and mid-expansion
- Non-buyers skew larger ($1M to $8M, four of them $2.5M+), multi-site or multi-entity, property or capital heavy, and in the middle of something: new sites, rebrand, accountant switch, new investor, development.
- Buyers ran one simple business. Ramy: two rinks, two entities, seven renovation projects.
- Four of eight non-buyers are hospitality or accommodation (Alex, Phillip, Kaveh, Michael). None of the buyers are. Add Ramy (roller rinks) and it's 5 of 9 in hospitality, leisure or accommodation. Hospitality content attracts them (Kaveh: "that's the ad that got me") but they haven't closed.

### 6. "Timing" was the excuse, not the cause
- Buyers had awful timing too: Suzie took the call from her father's palliative ward, Adam was heading into peak season, Xavier was flat out to Christmas. They bought anyway. Xavier: "At the time it happens, it's never the right time."
- When the pain is high and one person decides, timing doesn't stop the sale.
- Ramy proves the reverse: a booked calendar slot didn't save a low-pain prospect. He no-showed it.

### 7. Warm vs cold
- Buyers: all 4 had a setter conversation first and arrived believing they had the problem.
- Ramy arrived cold ("knew nothing", watched 10 min of YouTube). The call had to create the need, and it didn't stick overnight.

### 8. Price was never the objection. Payment shape was
- Buyers all took $880/week without blinking.
- Non-buyers asked to reshape it: Clayton to back-load it, Kaveh to pay in 2-3 instalments. Alex never heard the real price. Both reshape requests were turned down in favour of the weekly plan.

## Sales process gaps seen in the non-buyer calls
- Partner absent and no joint call booked (Phillip, Michael, Brad, Kaveh).
- Price came late (48 to 70 min in), and Alex never got it.
- Calls ran long (Ashita 95 min, Michael 77). Long software and slide monologues got "Yeah" in reply (Alex, Raj).
- Calls ended with a link in the Zoom chat and nothing in the calendar.
- Raj came to cut tax and didn't know what Gavin does. That should be screened out before the call.

## Sharpened ICP (buyers + non-buyers together)
Buy now: one owner who holds the money alone; break-even or thin profit; live tax pain (ATO debt or BAS shock); people-heavy service business; only help is a backwards-looking accountant; wants to understand it themselves; has hit "enough is enough".

Stall: profitable and comfortable; arrived cold; a partner or co-owner shares the decision and isn't on the call; already has a forecast, CFO, coach or franchise support; wants it done for them; mid-expansion or multi-entity; came to cut tax.
