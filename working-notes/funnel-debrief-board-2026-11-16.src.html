3 Sales a Week Funnel Board

1The Ads

Get the right people off Meta and onto the VSL page
1.5%/200 clicks
Daily / weekly ad spendA$315 / A$2,200
Click-through rate1.5%+
Cost per clickunder A$7
CPM (cost per 1,000 views)under A$90
Frequency (times seen)under 2.5
Levers, in priority order
  1. Refresh creative on a schedule. Retire ads once frequency passes 3.
  2. Kill and scale weekly. Turn off ads over your cost per booked call limit. Move budget to the winners.
  3. Hooks in your buyer's own words, taken from sales calls and applications.
  4. Test 3 new hooks a week on a proven ad body.
  5. Call out the buyer in the first line: who it's for, what size of business.
  6. Match the ad to the page. Same promise as the VSL headline.
  7. Mix formats: talking head, client story, static, text on screen.
  8. Test audiences: broad against lookalikes of buyers. Exclude current clients.
  9. Make sure clicks land. A slow page loses people between the click and the page view.
  10. Hold through expensive seasons. Judge on cost per booked call, not CPM.

2The VSL

Turn page visitors into booked calls
4.5%/9 booked
Page views200 a week
Applications11 a week
Calls booked9 a week
Booked call rate4.5%+
Cost per booked callunder A$300
Levers, in priority order
  1. Sharpen the headline and first 30 seconds. That's where most people leave.
  2. Chase applications that don't book. Text or call within the hour.
  3. Retarget visitors who didn't book with short clips and client stories.
  4. Proof near the button. Real client results with numbers.
  5. Answer the top 3 objections on the page.
  6. Say what happens next. A clear call to action, repeated down the page.
  7. Shorter application form. Only ask what the call needs.
  8. Slots in the next 48 hours showing on the calendar.
  9. Check it on a phone: fast load, captions on, easy button.
  10. Test one change at a time, once traffic is steady, so you know what moved.

3Appointment Engine

Get booked people to turn up ready
70%/6 showed
Calls due9 a week
Calls showed6 a week
Cancelled or rescheduledunder 2
No showsunder 2
Show rate70%+
Levers, in priority order
  1. Personal touch within 5 minutes of booking. A text or voice memo from the person taking the call.
  2. Book calls inside 48 hours. The longer the wait, the more they cancel.
  3. Reminder run 24 hours, 2 hours and 15 minutes out, by SMS and email. Ask them to reply "YES".
  4. Call anyone unconfirmed on the morning of the call.
  5. Pre-call video: what happens on the call plus one client story.
  6. Pre-call homework, like bringing last month's numbers. Invested people turn up.
  7. Rebook no-shows within 10 minutes with a direct link.
  8. A clear calendar invite from a real person, with the link and what to expect.
  9. Find who shows least (by business size, ad, time slot) and give them extra care.
  10. Log every cancellation reason and fix the pattern.

4The Sales Call

Turn calls sat into paying clients
50%/3 converted
Calls sat6 a week
Sales3 a week
Close rate50%
Work in progress: BAMFAMall
Not closed: nurture piece sentall
Levers, in priority order
  1. Review one recorded call per closer each week. What worked, where it stalled.
  2. Read the application before every call: their numbers, their words, their pain.
  3. Work their real situation on the call. Show them their next right move.
  4. BAMFAM every work in progress: book a meeting from a meeting before the call ends.
  5. Objection log with your best answers to price, timing and "I need to ask my partner".
  6. Get the decision maker on the call. Ask at booking.
  7. One call structure every closer follows.
  8. Role-play the hardest objection once a week.
  9. Nurture every "not now" with a useful piece of advice or a clear next step.
  10. Track close rate by closer and by lead source to see where the wins come from.

How we pick the week's focus

1. One thing at a time. Judge rates on a rolling 4-week average. 2. Work right to left. Start closest to the sale. The first stage under target is the focus. 3. Pick one or two levers from that stage. One owner, a due date, a clear finish line.

The weekly debrief

ObjectiveWhat was the target? 3 sales, plus each stage's number.
ResultWhat did we actually achieve? This week and the 4-week average.
ReasonsWhy? Use the stage numbers above to find where it leaked.
ResponseWhich one do we tackle first? Lever, owner, due date.
