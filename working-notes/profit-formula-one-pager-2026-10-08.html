The Profit Formula™

Seven numbers that explain how your business makes profit. And what to do next.
What it is

Profit is an outcome, not something you do. Like losing 10 kilos, you can't do it directly. You change the things in front of it and the result follows.

The formula goes one layer deeper. Income becomes price × volume. The 200 lines in your P&L become five buckets, because people, stock and marketing are each managed differently. Seven numbers: the recipe your profit comes from.

Why we do it
  • 1Know your business. Know your seven numbers off the top of your head and every monthly result shows you straight away where you're winning and where you're losing.
  • 2Find your next right move. Each number is a lever. Your time and money are limited, so put them where profit moves most. Hire, fire, buy the ads? Ask how it changes your formula.

How to fill it in (work backwards, easiest first)

You need: your Clarity Report (last 3 months of actuals) open.

NumberWhat goes in itWhere to find itWatch for
1
7 · Overheads
$ / month		 Everything else: software, insurance, phone, fees, subscriptions. Overheads line. Add the last 3 months, divide by 3. A huge or negative month? That's an accounting adjustment, not your business. Leave it out.
2
6 · Property
$ / month		 Rent and premises. Zero if you work from home. Property line. Should match your lease. A zero then a double month means rent was paid late. Write what you actually pay.
3
5 · Marketing & Growth
$ / month		 What you invest to grow: ads, brand, lead generation, growth hires. Marketing & Growth line, 3-month average. Zero or $500 is fine. Round $500 up to 1.
4
4 · Team
$ / month		 Everything people: wages, super, workers comp, recruitment. Team line, 3-month average. Weekly or fortnightly pay gives one "high" month a quarter. The average evens it out.
5
3 · Cost of Sales
% of sales		 Direct costs that rise and fall with sales: stock, materials, consumables. P&L ratios section: COGS %. Not the dollar line. Enter as a %. At 30%, a $10k job uses about $3k of materials.
6
Total revenue
$ / month		 Your average monthly revenue for the last 3 months. Revenue line, 3-month average. Don't put it in a box yet. Write it in the margin. You'll split it next.
7
1 · Price & 2 · Volume
$ and count		 Estimate whichever one you can (average sale, or number of jobs, clients or hours). Revenue ÷ that number = the other. Your POS, invoices sent, jobs done, hours billed. Prices all over the place? Pick a simple middle number. You'll still ask the same questions.
✓
Profit
check		 Run the formula. It should land close to your average operating profit. Operating profit line, 3-month average. Way off? Recheck Cost of Sales % and Team first. A loss is fine. Same tool gets you out.
