How to review a cash flow forecast

A simple order to work through any cash flow forecast, so you know if you can trust the numbers and what they're telling you.
Closing balance→ Income→ Expenses→ Closing balance

Start with the outcome. Check the inputs. Come back to the outcome.

1

Look at the closing balance first The next 13 weeks

Why 13 weeks? One quarter is long enough to see the weekly, fortnightly, monthly and quarterly payments all land on top of each other. Somewhere in there is the lowest week. That's the week we care about.

  • Is the balance positive or negative?
  • Which is the lowest week, and when does it hit?
  • How low does it get?
  • Is cash trending up or trending down?
  • Is there a healthy buffer compared to the cash running through the business, or is it tight?
2

Sense check the income

Compare what's actually been coming in against what the forecast says will come in.

  • What's the weekly average over the last 12 weeks? And the last 4 weeks?
  • What's the pattern? Weekly, fortnightly, monthly, or lumpy and all over the place?
  • Does the forecast match history? Higher, lower, same pattern, trending up or down?
  • Is there seasonality we need to question?
3

Sense check the expenses

Focus on what matters. Skip anything under about $1,000 a week. Only look at the bigger, chunkier numbers. For each one, compare the last 12 weeks and last 4 weeks of history to the next 13 weeks forecast.

BAS, IAS and GST

Usually the biggest, chunkiest payments. Is there a monthly IAS? A quarterly BAS? Are they all mapped forward?

Credit card

When and how much have they historically paid it off? Is the forecast payment a comparable amount?

Cost of sales

Same average as history? If income is forecast higher or lower, does this need to move with it?

Operating expenses

Same check. Is the forecast spend in line with history, and does it move with income?

Wages

Are we forecasting the same weekly average as the last 4 and 12 weeks?

Superannuation

Should now be a weekly figure that tracks wages, not a monthly or quarterly lump.

Loans

Is the repayment pattern clear in history and mapped forward on the same cycle?

Anything else

Any other patterns in the history that should show up in the forecast?

4

Come back to the closing balance Now you can trust it

The closing balance is only the result of every number above it. Once you're comfortable with the income and expenses, look again with fresh eyes.

  • Is it going up or down?
  • When is the lowest week, and how low is it?
  • How much cash does this business really have to work with?
  • What decision does this tell us to make next?

The one test behind every line: if the business is doing basically the same thing, does the forecast look like the history?
