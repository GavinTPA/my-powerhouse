# Beliefs, Assumptions, Bad Advice vs. What I Believe Instead

| Beliefs, Assumptions, Bad Advice | What I Believe Instead |
|---|---|
| Leave the numbers to your accountant, that's what they're for. | Your accountant runs your accounting, tax and compliance. They're not in the room every day when you decide to hire, raise prices, or spend money. You can outsource the accounting. You can't outsource the decisions. |
| Get yourself a good bookkeeper and you're sorted. | A bookkeeper makes sure the numbers are accurate. Accurate isn't the same as clear. They'll get the data right, they won't tell you what it means or what to do next. |
| Hire outside financial help, or rent a CFO, and they'll run point on it for you. | That's not empowerment, it's dependency with a bigger price tag. The day they're not available, you're stuck waiting. Build the capability into you and the business, not into someone you rent by the hour. |
| Open more bank accounts and your cash flow sorts itself out. | Buckets organise your cash. They don't tell you what's coming in, what's going out, or when the tight spot hits. A forecast does that. Accounts are just containers. |
| Don't worry about the numbers, just focus on sales, revenue growth will fix the profit problem. | More revenue doesn't mean more profit. You can grow revenue and grow your problems at exactly the same rate. Growth hides a broken model, it doesn't fix one. |
| If profit's down, cut costs. | Sometimes that's right. But trimming $20k off overhead is nothing next to fixing your pricing or your margin. Don't ask where you can save money, ask which lever moves the most money. |
| A good business should be making X% profit. | Says who? Every industry and every business model runs on different economics. Stop chasing someone else's benchmark and go find out what actually drives your margin. |
| Cash in the bank means the business is doing well. | $300k in the bank means nothing until you know what it has to cover. Tax, payroll, BAS, suppliers, that annual bill you forgot about. Bank balance isn't profit and it isn't health. |
| If you're profitable, cash takes care of itself. | You can be profitable and permanently broke. Tax, debt, stock, receivables and timing eat cash that profit never sees. Profit tells you the model works. Cash flow tells you if you can actually run it. |
| Your accountant will tell you if something's wrong. | By the time it shows up in a set of financial statements, you could've been making the wrong call for months. Waiting to be told is the most expensive way to run a business. |
| Your financials are a scoreboard, check them once a month. | A scoreboard tells you the score after the game's over. Your numbers are a control panel. Used properly, they tell you what to do next, not just what already happened. |
| If I can read a P&L, I know my numbers. | Knowing what the words mean isn't the same as knowing what to do about them. If your numbers aren't shaping your pricing, hiring or growth decisions, you're not using them, you're just reading them. |
| Set your budget once a year and refer back to it. | Reality moves three weeks later and the budget sits in a drawer. Don't set and forget. Forecast forward and keep updating it as the year actually plays out. |
| You need to track more KPIs. | 37 numbers on a dashboard and you still don't know what to do on Monday morning. More data isn't more clarity. Find the one number that matters right now and act on it. |
| Profit is just whatever's left over at the end of the month. | Profit shouldn't be a surprise you discover, it should be a number you design. Decide what you need, find the levers that produce it, then build towards it on purpose. |
