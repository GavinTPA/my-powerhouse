- 6 calls sat needs about 9 calls booked (70% show)

- At about A$250 per booked call, that's about A$2,200 a week, or roughly A$315 a day in ad spend

Every booked call is worth about A$3,080 in revenue (A$8,800 x 70% x 50%).

6. ROAS AND THE COST PER BOOKED CALL CEILING

ROAS = revenue from sales divided by ad spend.

Agreed targets:
- 4x front-end ROAS is the target
- 3x is the acceptable floor
- 2x is the kill line (half of every sale goes to Meta, not enough left to cover A$70k of costs)

How ROAS links to cost per booked call: max cost per booked call = (A$8,800 / ROAS) x the % of booked calls that become sales.

At target rates (70% show x 50% close = 35% of booked calls become sales):
- 4x ROAS -> cost per sale A$2,200 -> max cost per booked call about A$770
- 3x ROAS -> cost per sale A$2,933 -> max about A$1,027
- 2x ROAS -> cost per sale A$4,400 -> max about A$1,540

Important: these ceilings only hold if show and close rates hold. If show or close rate drops, the ceiling drops fast. Current cost per booked call (about A$257) is well inside the ceiling while Gavin and Melinda close at about 50%.

Two ROAS numbers to track:
- Contract ROAS: total contract value sold / ad spend
- Month-one cash ROAS: cash actually collected in the client's first month / ad spend (shows whether ads are paying for themselves in cash)


7. WHAT CPM MEANS

CPM = cost per 1,000 impressions (M is Latin for thousand). It is what Meta charges to show the ad 1,000 times, whether anyone clicks or not. It is the price of attention.

CPM rises when more advertisers compete for the same audience, when the audience has seen the ads too often, or when Meta rates the ad as low quality.

Late November and December are the most expensive months on Meta (Black Friday, Christmas). Expect CPM to rise over the next six weeks. Judge the ads on cost per booked call, not CPM alone.

9. HOW THE WEEK CUTOFF WORKS

Weeks run Monday to Sunday. Count every event in the week it actually happens:
- Page views and calls booked: the week the person landed and booked
- Calls due, shows and no-shows: the week the call was scheduled for, not the week it was booked
- A reschedule is not a no-show. It moves the call to its new week
- A no-show who rebooks: a no-show in week 1, a new call due in week 2
- Sales: the week they close, including follow-up calls

Weekly counts show activity. Rates (booked call rate, show rate, close rate, ROAS) use a rolling 4-week average, because with only about 9 calls a week one no-show moves the weekly show rate by about 11 points. That is noise, not a trend.

Once a month, do a people-based check: of everyone who booked that month, how many sat and how many bought.
