# Alex Hormozi: Gross Margins and 30-Day Cash Collected

## Key Moments

- **Learn the language of business** [0:00] - You only need about 100 terms, and almost all of them are a relationship between two numbers.
- **Net margin can never beat gross margin** [0:45] - If gross margin is 50%, a 50% net margin means zero overhead, zero marketing, zero admin. Gross margin sets the ceiling for everything else.
- **Selling out of your own wallet** [1:31] - Owners undercharge because they're short on money themselves, or because the work feels easy to them. Price off the value of the solved problem, not your effort.
- **The 70 / 80 / 90 money example** [2:40] - $100 delivery cost. At 70% gross margin you keep $233 per customer, at 80% you keep $400, at 90% you keep $900. Numbers that look close are worlds apart.
- **Commodity vs. decommoditised offer** [7:08] - If a customer can compare you side by side and pick the cheaper one, you can't hold 80%. Low margin is often the symptom; a commodity offer or broken sales process is the cause.
- **Rule of thumb #8: 30-day cash collected** [8:53] - Cash collected in a customer's first 30 days should exceed cost of delivery plus cost of acquiring them. Then each customer funds the next one.

## Summary

Hormozi's core argument: gross margin is the number that dictates everything else in the business. Net margin can never exceed it, so if you want a business that pays you well, the gross margin has to leave room for rent, admin, insurance, marketing and sales. His personal floor for service businesses is 80%, and he won't buy into anything below it [6:17].

The money example [2:40] is the memorable part. Going from 70% to 90% gross margin "sounds" like a small step but nearly quadruples the dollars kept per customer ($233 vs $900). He also notes the incremental margin above break-even is often pure profit, which is why small percentage shifts have outsized effects on what the owner can pay themselves [5:21].

He explains why owners undercharge (selling out of their own wallet [1:31]) and frames low margin as a symptom: usually of a commoditised offer or a sales process that isn't working [8:29].

The second rule [8:53] is about cash flow, not profit: collect enough cash in the first 30 days to cover cost of goods sold plus customer acquisition cost. Once that's true, growth funds itself because every customer pays back the cost of getting the next one.
