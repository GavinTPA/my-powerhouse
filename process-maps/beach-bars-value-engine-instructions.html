2. Instructions for the AI

If you are an AI assistant reading this document, these instructions are for you. A member of the beach bar team will work with you. Assume they know nothing about process maps. Your job is to help them produce two finished, agreed process maps, one at a time, following the method and standard in this document.

  1. Read the whole document first, especially the mapping convention (section 5) and the mock-ups (sections 8 and 9).
  2. Show the mock-up as your first draft. Draw it on screen if you can (Claude: an artifact or diagram; ChatGPT: render the Mermaid code or draw it). If you can't render diagrams, give the Mermaid code and tell them to paste it into mermaid.live to see it.
  3. List every assumption in the draft that they should check. Number them.
  4. Ask one question at a time. Use plain English. No jargon. Start with the biggest gap.
  5. Redraw the map after each change and keep the Mermaid code up to date, so the latest version is always available to copy.
  6. Give honest views. If a step looks wrong, missing or risky, say so and explain why. Don't just agree.
  7. Work through the three questions in order (section 4): first agree the flow, then mark the Power Stages, then put one name against each stage, then agree the KPIs.
  8. Only produce the final PDF once the person says the map is approved. Then add a short written summary (Power Stages, owners, KPIs) underneath or on a second page.
  9. Never invent numbers. If a KPI needs a figure (like an average spend), mark it "to confirm" and ask.

3. What we're doing and why

Every business runs on a small number of "value engines". These are the repeatable journeys that turn effort into money. For the beach bars there are two we care about first:

  • Growth Engine: how a person who has never been to us finds out about us and ends up as a guest in the venue.
  • Delivery Engine: what happens from the moment a guest is in the venue until they pay, leave and (we hope) come back.

The Growth Engine finishes where the Delivery Engine starts: "Guest in the venue".

Why bother? When the whole journey is on one page, you can see where we lose guests and where we lose money. Then we can focus on the two or three stages that matter most, make one person responsible for each, and track one number that tells us if it's working. Insights and actions, not reporting and over-analysis. 80% is good enough to start.

We've already done this for The Profit Analyst (Gav's other business). Section 7 shows that example so you can see what "finished" looks like.

4. The three questions

For each engine we answer these, in this order:

QuestionWhat it means
a. Which stages are truly critical?
We call these Power Stages.		Pick 3 to 5 stages where a small improvement makes a big difference to guests or money. Mark them with a gold star on the map.
b. Who is accountable for each stage?One name per stage. Not a team, not "everyone". Several people can do the work, but one person owns the result.
c. What tells us how each stage is performing?The numbers (KPIs) for each stage. Star the single number to watch for each Power Stage and write it in a gold tag beside the star.

5. Mapping convention (use this every time)

Every map uses the same shapes and colours so anyone can read any map.

Shapes

ShapeMeansExample
Oval / rounded pillStart or end"Potential guest", "Guest in the venue"
RectangleA task or activity"Greeted and seated", "Drinks served"
DiamondA decision with Yes/No branches"Table available?", "Shows up?"
Parallelogram (slanted box)Data or an input"Online booking form", "Social posts and reviews"
ArrowThe flow from one step to the next
Gold starA Power Stage, inside the box it relates to

Colours

ColourUsed for
Light blue, blue outline (#02A3FF)The main path
Light green, green outline (#12C870)Recovery and follow-up steps (saving a guest we were about to lose)
Navy (#002B4E)The start, and the long-term nurture list
Solid greenThe win (the end point we want)
Light greyEarly drop-offs, before we know who they are
Light redLost: left, cancelled, unhappy and gone
Light goldKPI tag beside a Power Stage
Dashed green lineLoops back to an earlier step

Layout rules

  • The main path runs straight down the middle, top to bottom.
  • "No" branches that are lost or exit go to the right. Recovery steps go to the left.
  • Every decision diamond has labelled branches (Yes/No, or the actual options).
  • Once we know who someone is (a booking name, a phone number), a "No" should go to a nurture list, not a dead end.
  • Keep box titles short (2 to 4 words) with a one-line subtitle if needed.
  • Include a key at the top of every finished map explaining shapes and colours.
  • Map what really happens today, not the ideal. We fix things after we can see them.

6. How we work (the process)

  1. Talk it through. Explain the journey out loud to your AI, as if training a new staff member.
  2. AI drafts the map on screen and lists every assumption it made.
  3. Check the assumptions one by one. Correct anything wrong. Add anything missing.
  4. Agree the flow. Read it top to bottom. Does every guest have a path? Does every "No" go somewhere?
  5. Mark the Power Stages (3 to 5 gold stars).
  6. Put one name against each stage.
  7. Agree the KPIs, with one starred number per Power Stage.
  8. Make the PDF only when it's approved. Send it to Gav.
Done looks like: a one-page PDF for each engine with the key at the top, the agreed flow, 3 to 5 gold stars, a KPI tag beside each star, and a short table of owners and KPIs. Plus the latest Mermaid code saved somewhere, so we can edit it later.

7. Worked example: The Profit Analyst Growth Engine

This is the finished map from Gav's coaching business, written out as text. Use it as the standard to aim for. It is a different business, so the steps won't match the bars, but the method is the same.

The flow

Prospect sees an ad on Meta → clicks (No: scrolls past) → lands on a video page → watches (No: leaves) → books a call (No: doesn't book) → fills in a form → revenue fits? (No: nurture list) → picks a time (No: setter calls to find a time) → call booked → confirmation SMS and a setter pre-call → right fit? (No: cancelled, nurture list) → morning-of SMS → shows up? (No: no-show follow-up, loops back to booked) → sales call → outcome: Buys (new client), No (nurture list) or Maybe (follow-up meeting booked, with its own show-up check and outcome) → New client, hand-off to delivery.

Power Stages and KPIs

Power StageStarred KPIWhy it matters
Lands on video pageCost per visit (plus volume)Every click is paid for. Cheaper visits means more guests for the same spend.
Books a call?Visit to booking rateA small lift here makes every booked call cheaper.
Shows up?Show rateA no-show wastes the most expensive thing in the engine.
Sales callClose rate, including follow-upsRevenue lands here. A "Maybe" with no follow-up is a sale we let go.
New client (end)Cost per new clientThe scoreboard for the whole engine.

Notice the four stage numbers multiply together: visits × booking rate × show rate × close rate = new clients. Lift each by 10% and you get about 46% more clients from the same spend. Look for the same multiplying chain in the bars: for example, guests through the door × average spend × return visits.

Accountability was one name per stage, for example "Show rate: Mikayla", even where several people make the calls.

Assumptions to check

  1. Guests find us through socials, Google Maps, reviews and walking past. What's missing? (Tourism sites, hotels, word of mouth, events?)
  2. There are two ways in: book online, or walk in. Are phone bookings, group bookings or functions separate?

  6. The engine ends at "Guest in the venue", which is where the Delivery Engine starts.
  7. Functions and events (weddings, birthdays, corporate) probably need their own Growth Engine map. Decide whether to map them now or later.

Assumptions to check

  1. It's table service. If guests order at the bar or by QR code, the order steps change.
  2. Drinks come before food. Is there a step for guests who only drink?
  3. There's a check-back after food, and staff offer another round or dessert. Does that happen every time?
  4. A manager steps in when food is late or a guest is unhappy. Who, and how do they know?
  5. We ask for a review and capture details (email, phone, loyalty) at the end. How, if at all, today?
  6. We have a list we use to bring people back (events, offers). Does it exist yet?
  7. "Regular guest" is the win. Decide what counts as regular (for example, 3 visits in 6 months).

10. Power Stage ideas to test

Don't start here. Agree the flow first. When you get to question (a), these are ideas to react to, not answers. Pick the 3 to 5 that matter most for our venues.

EnginePossible Power StagePossible KPI to star
GrowthSees or hears about the barNew guests per week; cost per new guest (if we pay for ads)
GrowthShows up? (bookings)Booking show rate
GrowthTable available? (walk-ins)% of walk-ins we seat, or walk-aways on busy days
DeliveryFirst drinks orderMinutes from seated to first drink
DeliveryExtra round?Average spend per head
DeliveryFood served% of meals out within target time
DeliveryReview requestGoogle rating and reviews per week
DeliveryComes back?Return visit rate

A useful check: guests through the door × average spend per head × visits per guest = revenue. The best Power Stages usually move one of those three numbers.

Known gap to flag early: if we can't currently measure a KPI (for example, return visits), write it down as a data gap. Don't drop the stage because it's hard to measure.

11. Making the final PDF

  • Claude: ask "Please turn the approved map into a branded one-page PDF with the key at the top, the Power Stage stars inside their boxes, and KPI tags beside them."
  • ChatGPT: ask the same. If it can't make a PDF, paste the Mermaid code into mermaid.live, export as PNG or SVG, and drop it into a document.
  • Name files clearly, for example "Beach Bars Growth Engine - Process Map.pdf".
  • Save the final Mermaid code with the PDF so we can edit the map later without starting again.
